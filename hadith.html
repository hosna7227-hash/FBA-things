The Hadith of Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara

The narration of Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara is one of the most remarkable hadiths in Islamic history, because in it, Prophet Muhammad ﷺ named ten specific companions by name and gave each of them the glad tidings of Paradise while they were still alive on earth. This is an honour not given to any other group of companions in a single narration.

The Narrator

This hadith was narrated by Sa'id ibn Zayd (RA) himself — one of the ten companions mentioned within it. Remarkably, when he narrated the hadith, he did not place emphasis on his own name or seek any praise, reflecting his deep humility even while relating his own promise of Paradise.

The Text of the Hadith

"Abu Bakr is in Paradise, Umar is in Paradise, Uthman is in Paradise, Ali is in Paradise, Talha is in Paradise, Az-Zubair is in Paradise, Abdur Rahman ibn Awf is in Paradise, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas is in Paradise, Sa'id ibn Zayd is in Paradise, and Abu Ubaidah ibn al-Jarrah is in Paradise."
— Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
(Sunan Abu Dawud 4649, Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3747, Musnad Ahmad)

Scholars of hadith have graded this narration as authentic (sahih), and it has been recorded by several major hadith compilers including Imam Abu Dawud, Imam At-Tirmidhi and Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal in their respective collections, with slightly varying chains of narration (isnad) but the same core meaning.

Why This Hadith is Unique

While many individual companions received the glad tidings of Paradise on separate occasions — such as Khadijah (RA), Fatimah (RA), Hasan and Husayn (RA), and Bilal ibn Rabah (RA) — the Ashara Mubashshara are distinguished because all ten were named together, in a single hadith, by the Prophet ﷺ himself. This collective mention makes the narration a distinct and well-known category in Islamic scholarship.

Lessons From This Hadith

This hadith carries several lessons for Muslims today. It shows that sincere faith, sacrifice, and service to Islam are recognised and rewarded by Allah — the ten companions came from different backgrounds (merchants, warriors, administrators) yet were united by their devotion. It also shows the importance of humility, as seen in how Sa'id ibn Zayd (RA) narrated his own inclusion in the promise without pride. Finally, it reminds believers that the promise of Paradise is tied to righteous action and unwavering loyalty to the truth, not merely to status or lineage.
