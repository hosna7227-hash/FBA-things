Frequently Asked Questions

1. What does Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara mean?

The Arabic phrase Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara (العشرة المبشرة) translates to "The Ten Who Were Given Glad Tidings," referring to the ten companions who received the good news of Paradise directly, a rare and unique honor among the Sahabah.

2. Who are the Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara (The Ten Islamic Luminaries)?

Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara (العشرة المبشرة) refers to ten companions of Prophet Muhammad who were given the glad tidings of Paradise while they were still alive on earth. They are: Abu Bakr, Umar Ibn Al-Khattab, Uthman Ibn Affan, Ali Ibn Abi Talib, Talha Ibn Ubaydullah, Zubair Ibn Al-Awwam, Abdur Rahman Ibn Awf, Sa'd Ibn Abi Waqqas, Sa'id Ibn Zayd, and Abu Ubaidah Ibn Al-Jarrah (may Allah be pleased with them all).

3. Why were these ten companions promised Paradise?

These companions were promised Paradise because of their exceptional faith, early acceptance of Islam, immense sacrifices, unwavering loyalty to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, and their outstanding contributions to the early Muslim community. Their promise of Paradise is recorded in an authentic hadith narrated by Sa'id Ibn Zayd (RA) himself — read the full hadith here.

4. How many of the Ten Companions later became Caliphs?

Four of the Ten Companions Promised Paradise became Rashidun Caliphs: Abu Bakr As-Siddiq, Umar Ibn Al-Khattab, Uthman Ibn Affan, and Ali Ibn Abi Talib.

5. Did other companions receive glad tidings of Paradise outside of this specific list?

Yes, several other companions were promised Paradise individually on different occasions, such as Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, Fatima Az-Zahra, Hasan and Husayn, and Bilal ibn Rabah (RA), but the Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara refers strictly to the group of ten mentioned together in a single, distinct hadith.

6. Who was the youngest member among the Al-Ashara Al-Mubashshara?

Sa'id ibn Zayd (RA) is generally recognized as the youngest member of the group, having embraced Islam when he was just a teenager alongside his wife, Fatima bint al-Khattab (the sister of Umar ibn al-Khattab).

7. Which of these ten companions was the first to draw blood in defense of Islam?

Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (RA) holds this unique distinction. During the early days of Islam in Mecca, while praying in a secluded valley, a group of idolaters insulted and attacked the Muslims. Sa'd defended the group by striking an attacker with the jawbone of a camel, shedding the first blood in Islamic history.
