5. Hazrat Talha Ibn Ubaydullah (RA)

طلحة بن عبيد الله (  ؓ  )

Hazrat Talha Ibn Ubaydullah (RA) was one of the ten companions promised Paradise during their lifetime. He was among the earliest Muslims and belonged to the distinguished six-member Shura Council appointed by Caliph Umar (RA) to choose the next leader of the Muslim Ummah.

Before embracing Islam, Talha (RA) was a prosperous merchant of Makkah. During one of his journeys to Ash-Sham, a Christian monk informed him about the imminent arrival of the final Prophet. Upon returning to Makkah and learning that his close friend Abu Bakr As-Siddiq (RA) had accepted Islam, Talha immediately went to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ and embraced the truth without hesitation.

His faith was tested from the very beginning. Because of his acceptance of Islam, he endured persecution and hardship with patience, never abandoning his belief in Allah or His Messenger. His steadfastness became a shining example for the early Muslim community.

Talha (RA) became famous for his extraordinary courage during the Battle of Uhud. As many Muslims were scattered across the battlefield, he remained firmly beside Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. He repeatedly placed himself between the Prophet and enemy attacks, using his own body as a shield to protect the Messenger of Allah.

He suffered numerous severe wounds while defending the Prophet. Historical reports mention that he received more than seventy injuries, and one of his hands became permanently disabled after stopping a deadly strike directed toward the Prophet ﷺ. Despite his injuries, he never retreated from the battlefield.

"Whoever wishes to see a man walking on the face of the earth who has already fulfilled his vow [of martyrdom], let him look at Talha Ibn Ubaydallah."
— Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
(Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3739)

Besides his unmatched bravery, Talha (RA) was widely recognized for his generosity. He spent his wealth freely in the service of Islam, cared for the needy, and continuously supported the Muslim community with sincerity and humility.

His remarkable faith, immense sacrifice, noble character and unwavering devotion earned him the honored title Ash-Shahid al-Hayy (الشهيد الحي), meaning "The Living Martyr." His inspiring life continues to remind Muslims that true faith is demonstrated through courage, sacrifice, loyalty and complete trust in Allah.
